# PVP — the permissionless value prop

*Provenance: upstream (`baseline-gtm-starter-kit`, carried in its ARTICLE and three
working files) — admitted under principle 1 as upstream-verified: the operator read it and
endorsed the test.*

**Strip the CTA. If the message still teaches the prospect something about their own
business, it passes. If it is pointless without the ask, it is a pitch.**

The test is mechanical: delete the last line, read what remains, and ask whether a
stranger would be glad they read it.

What fails the test, reliably:

- A fact they already know, said back to them ("I see you use [their obvious tool]")
- A compliment as an opener
- A question whose only purpose is to start a reply chain
- Anything whose value is contingent on them booking a call

## Two disciplines that apply under either instrument

**The why-now is datable.** A first touch names a reason to reach out *now*, and the
reason carries a date a stranger could check — a filing, a hire, a launch, an event. An
undatable why-now is a template pretending to be timing.

**Verify specifics.** Every specific about the prospect — the event, the name, the tool,
the number — is verified before it ships. A confident wrong detail is worse than no
detail: unknowns are marked unknown, never filled.

## Where PVP applies — and where it deliberately doesn't

PVP is one of **two first-touch instruments** this kit carries. The other is the
fixed-slot value-prop-testing template in Michael's TAM framework
(`motions/tam/skeleton.md` §copy): a deliberately plain, never-rewritten template where
the only things tested are the value proposition and the list. By PVP's own test it is a
pitch — on purpose; it optimizes yield discovery at volume, not per-message value.

Neither is wrong until a test says so: `experiments/001-first-touch-formula.md` is the
open experiment, and an account declares which instrument a campaign runs under. The one
settled rule: **never blend the two inside one campaign.**
