## The central law [R]

**Recurring impact precedes recurring revenue.** Revenue is the output of a system
that delivers impact to the customer — and revenue *recurs* only when the impact
recurs. A system designed to extract a close produces one-time revenue and a leaky
bucket; a system designed to deliver recurring impact produces retention, expansion,
and advocacy as consequences rather than campaigns.

What this already grounds in the kit: the retainer rung of any offer ladder
(continuity = impact recurring, priced), the *learn* pillar (a system that cannot see
its delivered impact cannot repeat it), and the bowtie's whole right side.

## The bowtie, in its home rung [R]

The bowtie is van der Kooij's signature model: the customer journey as two funnels
joined at the commit — acquisition on the left (aware → educate → select), then
onboarding, then the right side where recurring revenue actually lives (adoption →
impact → expansion → advocacy). The design claim: **half the revenue system operates
after the close**, and an architecture that only instruments the left half is
measuring a funnel, not a revenue system.

Kit status unchanged: the bowtie is **held, not adopted**
(`chain-of-operations.md` §Rung 3) — there is no post-close subsystem in operation
for it to describe. This rung explains *why the model matters*; whether it has anything
here to describe decides when it enters.

## Revenue is math [R]

The system is measurable end to end: volume and conversion at every journey stage,
time-to-move between stages, and price — growth as a function of those factors, not
of effort. The design consequences the kit already lives by, now grounded at their
rung: every motion declares its measurable stages; a stage nobody can measure is a
stage nobody can debug; and the *shape* of the math is architecture while every
actual number is an account value (`principles.md` §3 — the two rules compose, they
do not compete).

## Motions are designed, per segment [R]

A GTM motion is a designed combination — a segment, reached through a channel, with
an offer model — and different segments warrant different motions rather than one
motion stretched across all. Already alive in the kit as tier-as-budget
(`lexicon.md` §Tier), one-campaign-workflow-per-session (`principles.md` §Load
discipline), and frame-and-filling declared per account. The rung-2 statement is the
general form: **a motion that cannot name its segment was never designed — it was
just launched.**
