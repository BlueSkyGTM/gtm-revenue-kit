## The critique that earns its place [V — ch. 3 recap]

The book characterizes three older methodologies and then names what they miss:

| Methodology | What it focuses on |
|---|---|
| **Solution selling** | Features and benefits |
| **Consultative selling** | Value, and solving a *known* problem |
| **Provocative selling** | Challenging the status quo and *establishing* the problem |

Its objection: **none of them was built for a recurring-revenue business.** Each
addresses a slice of the relationship, so each comes apart when the parts on either
side of the slice are missing.

**Why this matters to this operation [operator, 08-15]:** it named a real weakness in
this firm's own proposal. Selling a point fix — a site, an automation — is solution
selling, and it survives exactly as long as nobody asks what happens upstream or
downstream of it. This is the reasoning behind *diagnose in eight, treat in bundles*
(`conceptual-framework.md`) and behind the diagnostic-led offer: a diagnosis is
inherently about seams, which is what a slice-shaped method cannot see.

## The six stages [V — the method figure]

One journey, named from the customer's side, each stage carrying the customer's own
reaction as its marker:

| Stage | The customer's state |
|---|---|
| **Awareness** | *Argh!* — an issue is impacting the business |
| **Education** | *Aha!* — there is a solution to this problem |
| **Selection** | *Wow!* — this company can really help us |
| **Onboard** | *Yes!* — delivered on time, on budget, as promised |
| **Impact** | *Yeah!* — impact is actually being achieved |
| **Grow** | *OMG!* — where else can we create impact? |

**The growth loop** returns from *Grow*: impact achieved becomes the input to the next
expansion rather than the end of the sale. The older methodologies are drawn as spans
across these stages — solution selling covering the narrowest band, consultative
wider, provocative wider still — which is the critique made visual rather than argued.

**The uniform-language claim:** one methodology for **all** customer-facing roles —
marketing, prospecting, selling, customer success, account management — so that the
handoffs between them stop being translations. The book carries this into a common
data model in the following chapter (not supplied; not claimed here).

## Figure 1.1 — the pile, drawn [V]

The book opens with a picture of what an organization looks like when it grows without
design: a jumble of disconnected boxes — onboarding program, training, SKO, territory,
org structure, strategy, go-to-market, playbook, growth hacker, hire more, sales ops,
tools, deals, CRM, pitch, tactics, price, superstars, comp plan, quota, reports, data,
dashboards, more tools, processes, and two consultants bolted on the sides — captioned
**"in a superstar culture sales, organizations grow with little design."**

Its argument against that culture: superstars are hard to find, hard to retain, and
unpredictable in performance; land-and-expand shrinks the initial deal, so the model
that needed a closer no longer pays for one; and no scaling company can run on 20% of
its people producing 80% of its revenue. The alternative it proposes is a **science
culture** — designed, measured, repeatable.

**This is the estate's no-silos law with a picture attached.** The corpus wing exists
to prevent exactly the state that figure depicts, and this is the strongest external
evidence the kit holds for it (`estate.md`; the corpus wing's intake law).

## What this means here **[the drafter's derivation — argue with it]**

- **The six stages and the eight functions are orthogonal, not rivals.** The stages
  are a *customer journey* — where the buyer is. The eight are *system functions* —
  what the business must do. A journey stage is served by several functions at once,
  and a function shows up at several stages. Rough correspondence, not a mapping to
  enforce: awareness/education ≈ reach and offer landing · selection ≈ move ·
  onboard/impact/grow ≈ the post-close half this kit does not yet have · the growth
  loop ≈ *learn*, made structural.
- **The growth loop is the flywheel** (`chain-of-operations.md` §Rung 3) arriving from
  a sales lineage instead of an architecture one.
- **"One language across every customer-facing role" is the thread.** In a firm of one
  operator the roles collapse into a single seat, which removes the handoff problem
  and leaves the sequencing problem — the stages still have to happen in order, and
  the seams still leak.
- **What is deliberately not taken:** the stage vocabulary does not replace the eight
  in this kit's own reasoning. Two frames, one home each; the lexicon prevents the
  conflation.

## Siblings — noted, not scoped

*Sales as a Science* is book 1 of the Sales Blueprints series; the remaining volumes
are role-specific (fundamentals, SDR, account executive, customer success and account
management), and *Blueprints for a SaaS Sales Organization* is the org-design volume
Figure 1.1 cites. **One volume covers the sales portion for this operation** — we are
GTM engineers, not a sales academy. One pointer worth keeping: **the customer-success
volume is the source to reach for if a post-close subsystem is ever built**, which is
the condition the bowtie has been waiting on.
