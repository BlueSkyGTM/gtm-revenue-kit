# Lexicon — terms that mean something specific here

*Provenance: the operation's semantic layer, distilled to the entries that prevent
conflations observed in live work. Operator-proven (principle 1) — each row exists
because reading it the other way produced a real error. Swap-test clean.*

**Read when a term in a request could go two ways.** The failure this prevents is a
fluent answer to the wrong reading, which is invisible from inside the answer.

The role's two layers — revenue engineer owns the system, GTM engineer executes within
it — are defined in `revenue-engineering.md` §The definition. Two usage rules follow from
that definition and live here: the narrow reading is **not an error**, so never correct
someone using it; and where the system layer is meant, say so rather than switching
definitions silently.

## The discipline and the roles — never conflated

Full definitions: `revenue-engineering.md` (the definition, the thesis) and
`chain-of-operations.md` (the ladder). These rows only disambiguate.

| Term | Here it means | Read it this way and something breaks |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue engineering** | **Revenue architecture + GTM engineering.** Ownership of the whole revenue system — signals, prioritization, messaging, channels, feedback — held as the capability to *deliver* any subsystem, not the obligation to operate every motion. Domain-agnostic; contains GTM engineering | As a synonym for GTM engineering. That reads the whole as its execution layer and silently drops ownership of targeting, offer, and learning |
| **GTM engineering** | The execution layer inside it: campaigns, lists, enrichment, tool wiring | As the whole discipline. (The narrow reading itself is not an error — see the usage rules above) |
| **RevOps** | Runs the existing engine: routing, hygiene, measurement, admin. The player, not the builder | As fused with GTM engineering. The roles borrow revenue-architect responsibilities from each other; neither overtakes the other (the 08-14 ruling) |
| **Revenue architecture** | Rung 2 of the ladder — the design layer's doctrine (`revenue-architecture.md`); van der Kooij's discipline | As rung 3, or as the *role* "revenue architect." A layer is not a job title. (A competitor also uses the phrase for funnel plumbing — one subsystem, not a rung; the account's offer research holds that trap) |
| **Systems architecture** | Rung 3 — the design layer; models are its vocabulary | As bare "architecture." The ladder names two, on purpose |
| **The merged seat** | The practitioner holding revenue-architect responsibilities *and* GTM execution. Revenue architects are GTM engineers now; not the reverse | As every GTM engineer. The seat is the smaller circle, and tooling serves the bigger one |
| **The eight pillars** | The same eight as rung 1's functions; *pillars* names their tool form — the conceptual framework (`conceptual-framework.md`) | As a ninth thing, or as an override. Framework judgment decides *within* the guardrails, never over a hard line |
| **Whitley's Duality** | A GTM system is **both a service and a business function** — delivered to clients *and* run by the operation on itself. Operator's name (08-21) for the operator's 08-14 claim; the ruling holds the two-arm test. Whitley supplies the eight functions, not this | As sequencing ("we serve ourselves first, clients later") rather than a standing property. Also: reading the name as Whitley's authorship — the eight are his words, the duality is not |

## Terms

| Term | Here it means | Read it this way and something breaks |
|---|---|---|
| **TAM (the artifact)** | The map of the addressable universe: the broad table, its ICP and anti-ICP rules, its suppression columns. Owned by the targeting subsystem | Treating it as a layer of the framework. It is the most load-bearing artifact in the system and still an artifact — centrality is not height |
| **The TAM pipeline** | The staged motion that *builds* the artifact (early stages) and *consumes* it (later stages) | Conflating it with the artifact. Different kinds of thing; one is a map, one is a process |
| **Anti-ICP** | Accounts excluded on **fit** grounds, stated positively with the reason inside the rule — "we do not sell to X because Y." First-class output, on the same artifact as the ICP | A low score, or an absence. An exclusion that exists only as absence cannot be debugged; one that never rejects anything is decoration |
| **Suppression** | Who must **not** be contacted on **consent** grounds — opt-outs, current clients, others' opportunities. Per-account, append-only, permanent | Merging it with anti-ICP (different grounds), or merging ledgers across accounts (consent given to one sender never transfers) |
| **Backbone** | The instrumented structure inside **one** business, covering the eight laws. **One per business** | Sharing one across businesses. What one business's motion learns travels as *method*, never as an operating instance |
| **Motion** | One repeatable sequence that moves a prospect forward. **Many per business, concurrent** | Reading backbone cardinality onto motions. "One backbone per business" and "one motion per business" sound alike; only the first is true |
| **Signal** | Dated evidence that predicts readiness, feeding exactly one dimension (fit / intent / relationship / timing) | Treating the choice of signal *framework* as doctrine. Reading signals is law-level; which framework does it is an instrument choice |
| **Channel map / funnel poster** | A taxonomy of traffic sources, capture assets, and conversion steps. A menu | A system. Audited against the eight laws, a typical one covers reach, capture, move-through, and follow-up — and is silent on targeting, offer, data retention, and learning. Those four decide whether any of the channels pay |
| **Tier** | A **budget** decision before a priority decision — how much research and how many touches an account is worth | A ranking alone. The research pass is the scarce resource; the tier is what rations it |
| **Instrument** | A tool subordinate to a revenue process, correctly discarded when the process changes | A product to be polished. High standards, yes — but optimizing the instrument at the expense of the process it serves is the recurring conflation |
| **Waste** | Leakage at a seam between two of the eight laws | Generic inefficiency. Naming the seam is what makes the waste checkable and tells you which subsystem owns the fix — placement, not admission; principle 1 is the admission test |

## Standing disambiguation rules

**Trace every decision to one of the eight laws.** What cannot be traced is either
unmoored or a gap worth recording. Both are findings.

**Neither a tool swap nor a product swap exits the discipline.** The question for any new
initiative is never "is this GTM work?" — it is *which laws does it need, and which
existing components does it reuse?*

**Match on capability, not vocabulary.** A source that never says "GTM" may describe this
discipline exactly; one that says it constantly may describe only the execution layer.

**Activity is not outcome.** Sends and touches are activity. Pipeline, win rate, and
expansion are outcomes.

**Three registers, one discipline.** Buyers hear *revenue solutions* — never a
discipline name. The market and peers hear *GTM engineers* — the market's word, not
fought; the differentiation rides on the remedy being holistic, not on a new noun. The
house thinks in *revenue engineering* — the internal name for the full stack, used
where the containment matters. The house word never leads in public copy.

**When a term could go either way, ask rather than pick.**
