# Play: One-to-One ABM

The highest-touch motion in the library: a single named individual — a founder-level target,
a dream-account executive — is selected deliberately, enriched to a complete picture in one
pass, and approached through whichever channel and gesture fits them specifically, including
physical gifting. The signal is operator judgment rather than an automated event, and the
build is about collapsing the tool-hopping that makes true 1:1 work too slow to do
consistently. It reaches the handful of people whose single yes is worth a whole campaign.

---

## The signal

**What fires:** the operator names a person. That is the honest description — this play's
trigger is a human decision that an individual matters enough for bespoke treatment, usually
because a Tier 1 account's champion is identified, a strategic deal needs an executive door
opened, or a relationship warrants a high-cost gesture.

**Where it comes from:** the operator's judgment, informed by the account's tiering (this
play should be reserved for targets at or above the band the account's
`context/scoring-model.md` defines for bespoke treatment). Often it fires downstream of
another play: a signal elsewhere in the library surfaces the account; this play takes over
for the one person who matters most in it.

**Decay:** none inherent — but the *occasion* chosen for the approach (their announcement,
their new role, their published take) decays like any event, and the approach should ride a
fresh one. The account's `signal-library.md` records this not as a scored row but as the
manual escalation path its Tier 1 treatment describes.

---

## Why it works

At the top of the market, volume mechanics invert. A person worth this play receives more
automated outreach than they can read, and filters it reflexively; what they cannot filter is
a gesture that visibly cost real time or money — a genuinely considered note, a physically
delivered object, a specific reference to their actual work. Costly signaling is the
mechanism: the effort is the message.

The operational insight is that 1:1 work dies not from unwillingness but from friction. When
assembling one person's full picture means a walk across five tools, sellers do it rarely and
inconsistently. Compress research-to-ready into one step and the bespoke motion becomes
routine instead of heroic.

The limit: this play does not scale and must not be scaled. Automating its personalization
converts a costly signal into a fraudulent one, and recipients at this level detect the
fraud. Also: a wrong fact lands worse here than anywhere in the system — the verify-specifics
standard in `foundations/pvp.md` at maximum stakes.

---

## The build

1. **Select deliberately.** The operator names the person and writes one sentence on why
   they are worth bespoke cost. If that sentence cannot be written, the target belongs in a
   tiered sequence, not here.
2. **One-pass enrichment.** From a single identifier (typically the profile URL), an
   enrichment pipeline — an orchestrator like Clay behind a one-click entry point such as a
   browser extension or shared table — returns the full picture at once: role and history,
   contact channels, recent public activity, mutual connections. The point of the build is
   that this is one action, not a tab tour.
3. **Deep research.** Run `motions/skills/account-research/SKILL.md` on their company, then read the
   person's own recent output (posts, talks, interviews). The output is a short brief in
   `accounts/<slug>/outputs/account-research/`, dated per the naming standard.
4. **Choose channel and gesture.** Match to the person, not to habit: a direct message where
   they are active, an email where they are formal, a messaging app where the relationship
   supports it, a physically shipped book or gift where a tangible gesture fits. Gifting
   compliance is real — the recipient's employer may cap or prohibit gifts; check before
   shipping, and log spend against the budget ceiling the account's `scoring-model.md` sets.
5. **Suppress and clear.** The account's `optouts.md` and CRM check apply to executives as
   to everyone.
6. **Send as a person.** From a real individual's account, ideally the most senior sender
   with a legitimate reason to write. No sequence tooling on the first touch; follow-ups are
   written, not scheduled.

## The message frame

The raw method behind this play specifies the sourcing and enrichment mechanics more fully
than the messaging, so this section is deliberately spare — the account should extend it
with what its own top-of-market touches teach. The frame that holds: the first touch must
prove bespoke effort within the first sentence — a specific, verified reference to *their*
work or situation that no template could produce — and then deliver something of standalone
worth: an insight about their business, a useful introduction, an object worth having. The
PVP standard (`foundations/pvp.md`) applies with the bar raised: stripped of any ask, the
touch should still be something they would mention to a colleague. The ask itself, when it
comes, is small and specific.

---

## Measurement

Sample sizes here are too small for rate math — the account's campaign gates (`scoring-model.md` §8)
explicitly do not apply to a motion this narrow. What is worth logging, per target, in the
account's outputs:

- Response obtained (any channel), and whether it converted to a conversation
- Cost per touch (time and spend) against the deal size that justified it
- Longitudinal: of the people run through this play, how many became open doors within the
  account's review horizon

Operator note: the raw capture behind this play carried no measurement method; the above is
the kit's own minimum. An account running this play at any volume should define its own
success criteria in its context before spending on it.

---

## When NOT to run it

- **As disguised volume.** Running this play on dozens of targets a week means it has become
  a sequence with expensive props. The costly-signal mechanism only works while it is
  actually costly and actually rare.
- **Below the tier that justifies it.** Bespoke cost against a mid-tier target is budget
  leakage; the tier bands exist to prevent exactly this.
- **On thin research.** A half-verified fact in a bespoke touch is the worst failure in the
  system. No brief, no send.
- **Where gifting is restricted.** Regulated industries and public-sector recipients often
  cannot accept gifts at all; the gesture becomes a compliance incident. Check first, always.
