## 2. The strongest current formulation is dual-path, not pain-only

The demand-based piece expands the method to **two segment types**:

- **Pain-Derived Segments (PDS)** — companies *currently* experiencing the systemic
  problem, found through **trailing indicators** (e.g., compliance records). This is the
  EDP method as recorded; the EDP concept lives here unchanged.
- **Demand-Qualified Segments (DQS)** — markets where structural demand **will create
  buyers**, found through **leading indicators** (demographic and infrastructure data),
  operating "2 to 4 years ahead of the RFP." The core insight: *"the condition that
  matters is the gap: load pulling away from capacity"* — market-level pressure that
  exists before any company feels internal pain.

Three supporting claims: institutions cannot quietly address structural needs ("a county
does not quietly replace a morgue" — formation leaves public records); the load/capacity
gap compounds and must eventually trigger buyer formation; upstream operation buys years
of lead time over competitors entering at RFP or permit stage.

**Consequence for 002:** the experiment as drafted tests PDS vs the firmographic map —
correct and unchanged. But DQS is a *third* segment definition, not a variant of PDS
(different indicators, different timescale, different decay). Record it now so it
neither contaminates 002 nor gets conflated with PDS later. If it ever enters the kit,
it is its own declared map type — the frame/filling rule already handles this.

## 3. The overwrite-prediction has no campaign data — from anyone

The evidence audit, stated as ratios:

- **DISCO case:** projections only. Five segments sized (~4,750 / ~3,000 / ~1,350 /
  ~1,000 / ~650 orgs), an EDP with a threshold (review-time ratio below 1.2×), and the
  $135M figure — which is **modeled ARR opportunity** ("our model suggests," "could
  potentially"), not results. **Zero reply rates, zero pipeline, zero revenue from an
  implemented campaign.**
- **Demand-based case:** one market (county forensic facilities) where six projects
  were traced through public bond records "exactly in the sequence the model predicts."
  That validates the *discovery mechanism* — the buyers were findable upstream — not
  conversion. No outreach results reported.
- **Third-party evidence:** none found. No practitioner outside Cannonball publishing
  pain-based campaign outcomes; the surrounding literature is conventional
  ICP/firmographic guidance.

**The honest summary:** mechanism plausible and internally consistent; worked analyses
exist; the prediction that pain-based overwrites firmographic frameworks is **argued,
not evidenced** — no published head-to-head, no conversion data, anywhere.

## 4. What this means for experiment 002 — the reframe worth keeping

002 is not following the field's evidence. **002 would be generating some of the first
real evidence the field has.** Two practical consequences:

- The decision gate as drafted (qualified replies per hundred sends, list decay over a
  quarter) is measuring exactly what the literature is missing — keep it strict, and
  the result is publishable-grade either way it lands.
- The hypothesis should carry the evidence state: the operator's direction toward
  pain-based rests on the *reasoning* (signal commoditization, pain outlasting
  structure) — which this research confirms is the strongest available support, because
  results do not exist yet. If 002 confirms, it is early evidence; if it refutes, the
  field's prediction loses its first real test. Neither outcome embarrasses the
  instrument.

One caution for the EDP threshold pattern: DISCO's "below 1.2×" is the shape an EDP
should take — a metric with an existential threshold — but it is a modeled example, not
a validated cutoff. When the operating account defines its own EDPs, thresholds are
account values with provenance "guess" until a campaign proves them (principle 3; the
labeling rule from the operator brief, `../tam/BRIEF.md`).
