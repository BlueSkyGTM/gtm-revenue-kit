# White-Label — Strategy

*Written 2026-07-25. This is the strategy. `abm-research-play.md` holds the tactics and is subordinate to this document. If the two ever disagree, this one wins.*

**Sequencing, so it stops being re-litigated: Bounce runs first, then Fenton pivots to this. The two campaigns never run at the same time.** Nothing below assumes concurrent operation, and the site can carry firm-facing positioning again by the time this starts.

---

## The two things the buyer actually fears

From Miriam, who pitched this originally and was right the first time. Everything downstream follows from these.

### 1. Looking incompetent in front of their own client

The nuance is the important part, and it is easy to get backwards. **The dangerous error is not the one an outsourcer makes. It is the one the firm makes because it is overloaded.** The missed deadline, the sloppy reconciliation, the return that went out wrong because the partner was working until eleven at night in March.

This inverts the sale. Fenton is not a new risk the firm has to evaluate and accept. **Fenton is insurance against a risk the firm is already carrying.** The status quo is the dangerous option.

Practical consequence: never open with "you can trust us." Trust language invites the trust objection. Open on the risk they already own.

Note who this bites hardest: **eponymous firms**, where the owner's surname is the firm name. Their personal reputation and the firm's are the same asset. Those firms already score highest in the model, so the highest-scoring segment is also the segment where fear 1 is sharpest.

### 2. Getting poached

The canonical white-label objection: the provider meets the client, then goes direct. A firm will rarely say this out loud, which is why it kills deals silently.

Fenton has three real answers and should give all of them before being asked:

- **Structural.** Not client-facing. No contact with the firm's client, ever.
- **Contractual.** A non-solicit offered up front and unprompted. Offering it before it is requested is worth more than the clause itself.
- **Capacity.** Fenton is deliberately small and has roughly three retainer slots. A provider with three slots cannot absorb a firm's book even if she wanted to. **Scarcity is the most credible anti-poaching proof available**, and it is true.

## Where we compete

**Overflow and cleanup work the firm cannot staff and does not want to refuse.** Not "outsourced bookkeeping," which is a category, not an entry point.

This is deliberately the narrowest wedge, and that is the point:

- It is the work firms most want gone. Lowest margin for them, real revenue for Fenton.
- Accepting help requires the firm to change nothing structurally. No firm restructures its practice to try an unknown provider. A firm will hand over one messy file it was going to turn down.
- It is the work where fear 1 lives. Messy files are what get dropped when a firm is full.

---

## Why we win there

Not quality. Every provider claims quality and the claim is unfalsifiable before purchase.

**One accountable, named, certified person.** The firm's real question is who actually touches the file and whether they will still be there in six months. Offshore providers rotate staff. Platforms churn or fold. Fenton is one person who answers for the work.

**Small enough that they matter.** A firm sending overflow to a large provider is a rounding error to that provider. To Fenton, one firm is a third of the entire book. That is a real difference in how the work gets treated and it is honest to say so.

**Same tooling, no translation.** Miriam is a QuickBooks ProAdvisor. So is most of the target list. That shared vocabulary removes an entire layer of explanation and is instantly legible to a QuickBooks-committed firm.

---

## When we win

**Busy season is January to April. Firms decide their capacity plan in October to December.**

A firm in March is drowning and has no bandwidth to evaluate, onboard or trust anyone new. A firm in October is planning, has budget attention, and is asking whether it can survive another season the same way.

**The buying window is autumn.** That is the calendar this campaign runs on, and it is the single most exploitable fact in the whole strategy. Arriving in February is arriving too late to be useful and too busy to be heard.

---

## How it compounds

**Three clients is not a revenue target. It is entry into a closed network.**

CPAs talk to CPAs. State societies, peer groups, referral relationships between firms that do not compete geographically. It is a small, conservative, high-trust professional community, which is exactly why cold entry is so hard and exactly why one satisfied firm is worth more than a hundred cold approaches.

The strategy is to buy into that network with three excellent deliveries and then stop cold prospecting. **If it works, client four arrives by referral and the 618-name list becomes irrelevant. If Fenton is still cold-prospecting at client ten, the strategy failed.**

## 1-1-1

**One niche.** Small QuickBooks-centric CPA and tax firms, roughly 2 to 15 people, taking on business clients with messy books. Weight toward eponymous firms, per fear 1.

**One offer.** QuickBooks cleanup and overflow, sold against the overload risk. Take the messy files off the desk before busy season. Concrete, bounded, low-commitment.

**One client.** An owner-operator whose surname is on the door, QuickBooks-committed, who turned away or fumbled work last busy season.

### On Xero — capability confirmed, and it sharpens the offer rather than splitting it

**Miriam has Xero capability and rates Xero the more ethical platform** (operator, 2026-07-25). Full research in `xero-migration-research.md`. Three findings change the picture.

**Do not sell migration.** Xero subsidises conversion to free, its preferred partner Q2X does QuickBooks Online to Xero at no charge, and third parties run $399 flat. The platform vendor gives the conversion away to win the customer, so any pitch built on "we will migrate you" competes with free and loses.

**Sell what the migration does not cover.** The transfer takes hours; pre-cleanup, validation and bank-feed reconnection take 2-3 weeks per file, quoted in the market at 20-40 hours of work, with consultant engagement around it at **$2,000-$5,000** and a failed migration costing roughly **$4,000** to repair. That is the same skill as the existing cleanup offer, so it does not violate 1-1-1 — it is the sharpest version of the offer already chosen.

**It supplies the niche, but not the Xero-shaped one.** Xero's own directory reports **617 Xero advisors in the entire United States**, against 5,925 in Australia and 4,799 in Britain, both far smaller countries. The Intuit sweep found 11,351 unique ProAdvisors across just 197 metros. Exactly **1 of our 617 firms** is a listed Xero partner. US firms have not adopted Xero, so "we migrate you to Xero" has almost no buyers here regardless of how good the platform is.

**The volume is QuickBooks to QuickBooks.** Desktop end-of-life forces firms to move their Desktop clients somewhere, Intuit is driving them to QuickBooks Online, and for a QuickBooks-native practice that is the path of least resistance. Dated triggers: Desktop 2023 support ended 2026-05-31, Desktop 2024 goes 2027-09-30, and the 2026-08-01 increase applied to **accountant-billed** subscriptions, hitting a firm's own resale margin rather than an SMB's bill.

So the niche is **"we clean up and validate your client files when you move a practice off Desktop."** It sells into a decision the firm has already been forced into rather than requiring a platform argument first, and it matches Miriam's ProAdvisor credential exactly, which 77 firms in the list share and will read instantly.

At book scale this is the big-ticket work: a firm moving 30 client files needs 30 files validated and reconciled, not 30 conversions.

**Xero's role is a differentiator in the room, not the offer.** Being able to say she can move a firm to Xero and rates it the more ethical platform is a genuine and unusual thing to offer a firm owner who resents being pushed around on price. It earns trust in a conversation. It is not what the campaign leads with. Still open: whether Miriam wants lumpy, deadline-bound project work at all.

## Gifting

Nothing physical can be sent through LinkedIn, so the gift is the only thing Fenton has in surplus: **expertise.** Two tiers, separated because Miriam's time is the constraint.

**Free and unlimited: the research findings.** What other small firms said about covering capacity. Costs nothing per additional recipient, and firm owners are isolated enough that peer comparison is genuinely valuable to them.

**Expensive and gated: a diagnostic on one real client file.** Miriam reviews one QuickBooks file and writes up what is actually wrong with it.

This is the strongest single move available for this sale. It **demonstrates** competence instead of claiming it, hands the firm something usable with their own client, and answers fear 1 directly by surfacing the errors that overload created. It also lets a firm see her work before committing to anything, which is the cheapest possible way past the trust problem.

**Gate it hard.** It costs Miriam an hour she does not have. Offer only after a conversation establishes real fit.

## Enrichment priority

Not more firms. The list is already two to four times larger than a three-client goal can consume. Two enrichments on the **existing** 618 are worth more than any amount of new sourcing:

1. **Certification cross-reference.** Both the Intuit ProAdvisor directory and the Xero advisor directory are public. Marking which firms are QuickBooks-certified, Xero-certified, or both splits the list into "Miriam's credential is instantly legible" (priority), "wrong fit today" and "the migration market, if that offer ever exists." The scoring doc flagged the ProAdvisor directory as the best available list source and it has never been used.
2. **LinkedIn activity.** Connection count as a proxy, per the 500+ filter. At roughly 40 touches a month, picking the right 40 beats owning another 600 names.

## What would falsify this

Answerable in the first handful of conversations, which is the real argument for starting sooner rather than perfecting the approach:

- **If firms consistently say their constraint is cost rather than capacity**, the wedge is wrong and Fenton loses to offshore providers on price permanently.
- **If firms say they would never let an outsider touch a client file under any terms**, the market does not exist at this size and no tactic repairs it.
- **If the overload framing does not land** — if firm owners do not recognise themselves in fear 1 — then Miriam's read of her own market is wrong, which would be surprising but is worth knowing early.
