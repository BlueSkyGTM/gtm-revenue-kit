## Evidence the category is real

1. **Xero certifies migration as a distinct specialism.** A platform vendor does not create a badge for work that does not exist or that clients do not ask for. That is the strongest single signal available and it costs nothing to observe.

2. **81 of the 286 records enriched in the last two runs hold the badge — 28%.** Roughly one in four Xero advisors is publicly advertising migration capability.

3. **Fourteen WL-1 firms are Xero-certified**, and at least one firm — AEH Accounting, Chris Rogers — sits on both lists. Those are practices straddling both platforms, which is what a firm doing migrations for a living looks like.

4. **The timing is real and was verified separately.** QuickBooks Desktop 2023 hit end of life 2026-05-31, Desktop 2024 follows 2027-09-30, and accountant-billed QBO pricing rose 2026-08-01. Clients are being pushed off Desktop whether or not anyone sells them a migration.

## The problem with using this list to test it

**A Migration specialist badge marks a supplier, not a buyer.**

These twenty firms are certified by Xero to perform migrations. They are the people a small business hires to move off QuickBooks. Selling migration capacity to them is selling ice to Inuits — and worse, it walks straight into the second fear Miriam named, because a certified migration specialist has the most to lose from a subcontractor near their clients.

Two further constraints, both established earlier in this campaign's research:

- **The migration itself is not the product.** Xero subsidises it, Q2X is free, and WOW BookSwitch charges $399. Nobody is paying real money for the file move.
- **The money is in the work around it**, and it is three separate lines, not one. Cleanup and data validation is **20-40 hours** per file. Integration setup and testing consulting is **$2,000-$5,000**. Repairing a failed migration is **~$4,000**. An earlier version of this report merged the cleanup hours with the consulting price and called the result a single $2-5K cleanup unit. That was wrong.

So the honest read is that migration is a real category with real timing pressure, but **WL-2's badge-holders are on the wrong side of the trade.**

---

## Recommendations

**1. Do not build the migration campaign against badge-holders.** They are certified to do the work themselves. Selling them migration capacity inverts the trade and provokes the poaching fear rather than defusing it. This is the main conclusion and it should override the instinct that a Migration specialist badge marks a good prospect.

**2. Sell the work around the migration, never the migration.** The file move is close to free: Xero subsidises it, Q2X is free, WOW BookSwitch is $399. Any copy leading on "we'll migrate you" competes with free. The paid lines are cleanup (20-40 hrs, priced on rate), integration consulting ($2,000-$5,000) and failed-migration repair (~$4,000). See `migration-cleanup-thesis.md` for how these stack into one offer.

**3. Test the demand side with 14 conversations, not credits.** Fourteen WL-1 firms are also Xero-certified, and at least one — AEH Accounting, Chris Rogers — appears on both lists. Those are practices already straddling both platforms and therefore already fielding migration requests. Ask them one question: is migration cleanup work you turn away? That answers feasibility directly instead of inferring it from badge data, costs nothing, and is a practitioner question Miriam is the right person to ask.

**4. If the category proves real, the target is the firm without the badge.** Three profiles, in order of promise: QuickBooks-native firms whose clients are being forced off Desktop and who would have to learn a new platform mid-season; the 14 dual-certified firms; and Xero advisors who have *not* advertised migration capability, which is the shape of a firm that gets asked and does not want the job.

**5. Screen location by hand before any outreach here.** The directory's state field is unreliable — Exonia is listed as "London, Texas" and is actually London, UK; DoubleRule is in Central Luzon, Philippines. Two of the top 20 are not US firms.

**6. If any subset deserves a closer look, it is the three L3 Certified Specialists** — Accounting Elements, Beyond Balanced Books, My Team Accounting. L3 plus the migration badge is the deepest platform commitment in the list, which makes them the best available read on what a serious Xero migration practice actually looks like and what it charges.

**7. Keep the two lists separate.** WL-1 is an overflow pitch to QuickBooks-native firms. WL-2 is a platform pitch to firms that mostly already do migrations. Different offer, different fear, different proof. The only place they touch is the 14 dual-certified firms, and those are evidence rather than volume — they belong to whichever campaign runs second.

**8. Park WL-2 until the Bounce campaign is live and the WL-1 question is answered.** Nothing in this analysis argues for spending here first. The category is real but this list is the wrong side of it, and the cheap test in recommendation 3 runs off WL-1 data anyway.
