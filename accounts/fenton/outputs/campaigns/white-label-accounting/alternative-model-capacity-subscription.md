# Alternative model — the capacity subscription

*Written 2026-07-26 as a deliberate counter-proposal to `migration-cleanup-thesis.md`. The operator asked for the alternative because I raised objections to the exit thesis. This is the strongest competing model I can build from the same evidence.*

---

## First, what I was actually objecting to

Worth being precise, because "unhappy with the proposal" overstates it.

Three objections were raised. **Two are now resolved:**

1. **The numbers were wrong.** That was my error, not a flaw in the thesis. Corrected, and the range survives on a better comp than the source had.
2. **Miriam has no Xero credential.** Resolved by the operator: if the consulting line appeals, she certifies within the week. That was the objection with real teeth and it is gone.

**One remains, and it is not fixable by argument:** there is no proof point and no timed file. That is true of any new offer, including this one. It is a reason to run a first engagement, not a reason to pick a different model.

So the honest position is that the exit thesis is good. What follows is not a rescue. It is the model that would win if one specific thing turns out badly.

---

## The alternative

**Sell accounting firms a monthly block of delivery hours. Not projects, not per-file. A standing subscription to Miriam's capacity.**

> 20 hours a month of QuickBooks delivery, behind your name, for a fixed monthly fee. Use them for cleanup, catch-up, month-end, whatever is on fire. Unused hours do not roll over.

That is the whole offer.

---

## Why this is a serious contender

**1. It monetises the one thing the research actually verified.**

The WL-1 site visits produced exactly one hard behavioural finding: the constraint is **time against a commitment**, not exhaustion and not poaching.

- Mattingly & Ott advertise *"100% On-time delivery"* across *"40+ Client books managed"* with financials promised *"by the 30th"*.
- Merritt promise a fixed **$250/month** across *"thousands of clients"* with two owners.
- Christina Rea sells **$125/hour** cleanup against retainers starting at **$225/month**.

Every one of those is a firm whose model breaks if throughput slips. **They do not need a project. They need a floor under their capacity.** A monthly block is the literal shape of that need; a per-file project is not.

**2. It is recurring by construction.**

The stated goal is three strong retainer clients. This model *is* retainers. The exit thesis produces project revenue and has to argue its way to recurring via "the firm relationship is the retainer" — which is true, but it is a second-order effect that has to be engineered. Here it is the primary structure.

Three firms on 20-hour blocks is a business. Three cleanup files is a month.

**3. It requires no new certification and no new platform.**

Miriam stays in QuickBooks, where the 30 years, the ProAdvisor cert, the audit-support background and the corrective-cleanup experience all live. Nothing is contingent on her certifying in a week, liking Xero, or the Xero side of a migration going smoothly.

**4. It satisfies the customer-service constraint completely.**

The firm is the only counterparty, permanently. Not "usually" or "mostly" — there is no mechanism by which an end client ever contacts Miriam, because the engagement is with the firm and the work arrives from the firm.

**5. It de-risks capacity for a solo deliverer.**

A one-person shop selling projects has a feast-famine problem and no way to plan. Sold blocks are known load. Miriam can say yes to a fourth firm or not, based on arithmetic rather than optimism.

**6. The trial is small.**

"Try ten hours this month" is a far lower-stakes first yes than "let me rebuild your client's file for $3,000." **The absence of a proof point hurts this model much less**, because the buyer is not betting a client relationship on an unproven supplier. That is the single biggest practical advantage.

---

## Where it is weaker, honestly

**No dated trigger.** The exit thesis has Intuit's 2026-08-01 price rise, Desktop end-of-life, and a platform exodus. This has nothing. "You seem busy" is not a why-now, and cold outreach without one is materially harder.

**Commodity risk.** An hour of bookkeeping capacity is comparable to other hours of bookkeeping capacity, including offshore ones at a fraction of the rate. The exit offer is a specialism; this is a resource. Price pressure is structural, and the only defences are the ProAdvisor credential and the audit-support background.

**It does not unify the two campaigns.** The 863 SMB records cannot buy a capacity block. This model abandons the Bounce list entirely and makes Fenton a firms-only business. That is a real strategic narrowing.

**Lower ceiling per engagement.** 20 hours at $125 is $2,500/month recurring, which is good — but a single failed-migration repair is $4,000 in two weeks. The exit thesis has more upside per unit.

**Trust before evidence.** A firm has to hand over live client work before seeing any output. The exit model can start with a discrete, bounded, historical file where nothing current is at risk.

---

## A third option, briefly

**The audit-ready close.** One service, all industries, but the service is year-end or audit-preparation books rather than migration.

Miriam's audit-support background is genuinely uncommon among solo bookkeepers and it is the credential nobody else on either list advertises. The work is seasonal, high-value, and **recurs annually by nature** — this year's client is next year's client with no re-selling.

Not developed further here because it has the weakest timing story of the three and no supporting research, but it is the model that best exploits the one credential Fenton has that competitors do not. Worth a look if both other models stall.

---

## Recommendation

**Do not choose. Sequence them.**

The two models are not competitors, they are different stages, and each one covers the other's weakness precisely:

| | Exit thesis | Capacity subscription |
|---|---|---|
| Dated trigger | **Yes** | No |
| Recurring revenue | No | **Yes** |
| Needs a cert | Yes | **No** |
| Survives no proof point | Poorly | **Well** |
| Unifies both lists | **Yes** | No |
| Ceiling per unit | **Higher** | Lower |

**Win the firm with an exit file. Keep the firm on a capacity block.**

A migration cleanup is the perfect first engagement — bounded, historical, urgent, dated, and nothing live is at risk while an unproven supplier proves themselves. Once it is delivered and the firm has seen the work, the conversation stops being "will you do a project" and becomes "how much of you can I have every month." The proof point that the exit thesis lacks is exactly what the subscription needs, and the recurring revenue that the subscription has is exactly what the exit thesis lacks.

That sequence also matches the stated goal. Three firms on standing blocks is three strong retainer clients, reached through an offer that has a reason to call.

**The one condition under which the alternative wins outright:** if the first timed file comes back at 40+ hours and the fixed fee will not hold, or if Miriam does not take to Xero after certifying. Either would make per-file exit work unprofitable or undeliverable, and the capacity model becomes the primary rather than the follow-on. Both are answerable by doing one file.

**So the next action is the same under either model, which is the useful part:** run the 14-conversation test with the dual-certified firms, and ask two questions instead of one. *Is migration cleanup work you turn away?* and *would you buy a standing block of hours?* Same fourteen calls, both hypotheses tested, no credits spent.
