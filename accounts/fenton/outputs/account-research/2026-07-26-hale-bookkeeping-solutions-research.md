## Why this account

**The ProAdvisor confirmation plus the QuickBooks reseller page make this the most certain QuickBooks-native firm in the top 20.** Reselling QuickBooks products is something only a ProAdvisor does — it confirms the certification independently of the Airtable field.

**Fixed monthly pricing is the pressure point.** She charges a fixed cost per portfolio. Real estate investor books scale with property count, and a client who buys three more properties mid-year generates more work at the same price. Fixed-fee firms absorb that silently until they cannot.

**The vertical focus cuts both ways.** A single-niche firm is referred a lot of near-identical work, which is good for volume and bad for capacity. It also means the work is standardised, which makes it unusually easy to hand to a subcontractor: the same chart of accounts, the same transaction shapes, every time.

## The Angle

**Why now:** She charges a fixed monthly fee per portfolio to real estate investors, a client type that adds properties. Fixed price against growing scope is a margin squeeze that only shows up as her own unpaid hours.

**Why us:** Real estate investor books are standardised — same account structure, same transaction patterns. That makes them the easiest kind of work to hand to a second pair of hands without a long handover.

**Hook:** The niche and the pricing model together. A fixed fee per portfolio is a deliberate choice worth asking about: what happens when a client goes from four doors to twelve.

**Secondary angle if overflow lands flat:** ask whether she takes on cleanup at all. Nothing on the site says she does, and a QuickBooks reseller with a real estate niche will be asked for it constantly.
