## Why this account

**The published rate card is the whole opportunity.** Cleanup sells at $125/hour and monthly retainers start at $225. That means a single 20-hour cleanup is worth roughly eleven months of a base retainer to her — and cleanup hours are the ones that cannot be batched, automated, or deferred.

Every cleanup she sells is revenue she has to personally deliver at the expense of the recurring book. A subcontractor who takes the rebuild lets her keep selling the $125/hour line without capping it at her own calendar.

She also sells **QuickBooks training** at $200 for two sessions, which tells you she is being asked to teach clients the software as well as run it. That is another draw on the same hours.

## Competitive context

She sells cleanup herself, so the approach must be capacity, never capability. Her brand voice is warm and informal ("rad", "we've seen worse") — a stiff or corporate approach will read as a bad fit.

## The Angle

**Why now:** Cleanup is priced hourly at $125 while her recurring book starts at $225 a month. The economics push her to say yes to cleanup, and her own hours are the only place those hours can come from.

**Why us:** Cleanup is the one line she can hand off without a client noticing. It is bounded, historical, and finished. She keeps the client, the rate, and the relationship.

**Hook:** Reference the price list directly — she published it, so using it is fair and specific. The question worth asking is what happens to the monthly clients in the weeks a large cleanup is running.
