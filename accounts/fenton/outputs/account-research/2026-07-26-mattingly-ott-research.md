## Why this is the strongest account on the list

Four things stack, and they stack in the one direction that creates a buyer:

1. **40+ books is past the point where one person closes a month comfortably.**
2. **A hard, published deadline.** "In your inbox by the 30th" is a promise with a date. There is no slack in it.
3. **100% on-time is advertised.** They have made punctuality the product. A missed close is not an inconvenience, it is a broken claim on their homepage.
4. **They already sell cleanup as a separate line.** Cleanup is lumpy, project-shaped, and arrives without warning. Every cleanup they win competes directly with the monthly close they promised to deliver by the 30th.

That is a structural capacity trap, not a guess. Add the podcast, which is time spent on marketing rather than delivery.

## Competitive context

They *are* a cleanup provider. That cuts both ways: it means they understand the work and will not need educating, and it means they may read an approach as competitive. Frame it as capacity behind their name, never as an alternative to them.

## The Angle

**Why now:** They advertise 100% on-time delivery on 40+ client books with a hard close by the 30th, and they sell cleanup projects alongside it. Cleanup work arrives unpredictably and eats exactly the days the monthly close needs. That collision is the pitch, and it is visible on their own homepage.

**Why us:** A cleanup rebuild is the most subcontractable thing a bookkeeping firm does — it is bounded, it is not client-facing, and it does not touch the monthly rhythm. Miriam takes the rebuild, Ott keeps the close and the relationship.

**Hook:** Their own promise, used back. Something along the lines of noticing they guarantee books by the 30th across 40+ clients while also taking on full rebuilds, and asking which of the two gives when a big cleanup lands in the same week as close.

**Do not:** lead with pricing, or with anything implying their books need fixing. They sell cleanup. They are the competent party here.

**Recommended sender:** Miriam. This is a practitioner-to-practitioner question about month-end mechanics and it will not survive coming from a salesperson.
