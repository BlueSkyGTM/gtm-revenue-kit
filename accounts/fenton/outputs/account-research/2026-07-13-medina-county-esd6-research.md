## Company Snapshot
Medina County Emergency Services District No. 6 (MCESD 6) is a **Texas special district (political subdivision), not a for-profit business** — it exists under the Texas Health and Safety Code to fund and provide rural fire prevention, EMS, and emergency ambulance services. Based in Hondo, TX (P.O. Box 201, Hondo 78861), it serves the Hondo/Yancey area of Medina County, west of San Antonio. Central timezone. (Verified: mcesd6.com, SAFE-D member directory.)

It is a **contracting/funding body**, not a fire department itself — it collects property tax and contracts the actual response out to volunteer fire departments. (Verified.) The district is funded by **property taxes on local taxpayers**, not sales revenue: the 2026 budget shows **$1,448,998 in Maintenance & Operations** (up from $1,382,891 in 2025), $0 debt service, all property-tax supported. Published 2026 change: revenue +$340,107 (17.1%), expenses -$553,668 (-21.9%). (Verified: medinaesd6.com/budget-information.)

**Recent public event (material to the angle):** In Oct-Nov 2024 the historic Hondo Volunteer Fire Department (115 years, est. 1909) terminated its contract with MCESD 6, effective Nov 21, 2024, citing **lack of funding, budget disputes (air packs, extrication tools), and transparency/leadership concerns** with the ESD board. MCESD 6 then contracted neighboring ESD 1 to provide 24/7 professional fire coverage out of the Hondo station. (Verified: KENS5, KSAT, Devine News, Firehouse, Nov 2024.) Takeaway: this board has been publicly scrutinized over how it spends taxpayer money and has real budget pressure right now.

Governance: five appointed (not elected) commissioners meet monthly (4th Tuesday, 6:30 PM). Meetings and agendas are public. (Verified: mcesd6.com.)

Chosen: **paying-more-getting-software** (the Aug 1 QuickBooks increase), from `rollout-a.md`. **Why now:** Intuit raised QuickBooks prices again effective August 1 (Plus now $140/mo, Advanced $340/mo), and each account picks up the new rate at its own renewal. This district runs on a fixed, property-tax-funded budget and was just publicly challenged over how it spends that money after the Hondo VFD split. A software increase on taxpayer dollars is a defensible reason to look at the line item when the next budget cycle comes around.

**Why us:** A district paying Intuit for bookkeeping help is paying a rising software bill plus a bookkeeping service from the same vendor. One independent, QuickBooks-Certified person doing the bookkeeping and payroll, at a flat cost the board can put in a budget line, is easier to defend in a public meeting than an escalating subscription. No platform migration — the books stay in QuickBooks, only who does the work changes.
