## 2. Battlecards

Translated from `context/competitor-radar.md` into what Miriam says. Intel lives there, not here.

### vs. Status quo (doing it themselves)

- **Comes up as:** "We handle it in-house" or "I just do it myself."
- **Where we win:** they can name a specific cost, a caught error, a price shock, or the hours it eats.
- **Where they genuinely win:** zero switching cost, full control, already paid for.
- **Trap to set:** *"What is it actually costing you in hours a month right now?"* Their own number does the selling.
- **Landmine:** do not imply they have been careless. The books fell behind because the business grew.

### vs. QuickBooks Live

- **Comes up as:** "We already pay Intuit for bookkeeping help."
- **Where we win:** one consistent person instead of a rotating pool, no stake in the software, payroll included rather than excluded.
- **Where they genuinely win:** native to the file, no new vendor, often cheaper on paper.
- **Trap to set:** *"How has the turnaround been, and have you had the same bookkeeper the whole time?"*
- **Landmine:** do not attack Intuit broadly. The prospect chose QuickBooks and may still like it. The argument is about who does the work, not the software.

### vs. Bench

- **Comes up as:** "Aren't you basically Bench?" Usually unprompted, from someone who read about the 2024 shutdown.
- **Where we win:** a named accountable person cannot lock you out of your own records.
- **Where they genuinely win:** national brand, own software layer.
- **Landmine:** never lead with Bench. Only respond if raised. Leading with a competitor's failure reads as fear.

## 3. Discovery talk track (the free books review call)

**Open (60 seconds).** Thank them for the application, read back the one line they wrote in "what made you reach out," and confirm it. That line is the whole reason they called.

**The question that sets the price** (from `context/pricing-strategy.md`, do not re-derive):

> *"What are you paying Intuit today, the QuickBooks subscription plus any bookkeeping help like Live or Expert Assisted?"*

**Then, in order:** how far behind, what broke last, who touches the file, and what has to be true in 90 days (a filing, a lender, a sale).

**Close on the review, not the retainer.** The deliverable of this call is what to fix first. The engagement is the next conversation. Trying to close both in one call is what makes a trust sale feel like a pitch.

## 4. Objection matrix

| Objection | Root concern | Reframe | Proof that closes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| "What does this cost?" | Price | Cost tracks the backlog and the monthly volume, so the review comes before the number. Anchor against their Intuit bill, not against a cheap bookkeeper. | Their own invoice, split into software versus bookkeeping. QB Live Full Service runs $300 to $700 a month plus a $500 to $800 cleanup to start. |
| "We already pay for QuickBooks Live." | Status quo | The help and the automation causing the errors come from the same company, inside the same file. | Reviews report QB Live bookkeepers running 4 to 5 months behind, and one documented $1,500 cleanup fee to fix their own errors. |
| "Can you work in my existing file?" | Risk | Nothing migrates. No new software, no new login, no workflow change. | The switch is who does the work. Every target account is already a QBO subscriber. |
| "How do I know my books are safe with one person?" | Risk | A named person is the accountability, not the risk. A platform can close between one tax season and the next. | ProAdvisor credential, 30 years. Bench cut 12,000 businesses off from their own records in December 2024. |
| "I am not sure it is bad enough to fix." | Timing | The review is free and tells them either way. If the books are fine, they get that in writing and walk. | The four checks in the review. Nothing is owed if they stop there. |
| "Let me think about it." | Timing | Give them one thing to do that needs no decision. | *"Pull your last Intuit invoice and split the two line items. Call me either way."* |

*Live delivery and tone are out of scope here. This is the written answer set.*

## 5. Application to quote, before the call

The form already collects the two fields that pre-band a quote. Map them per `context/pricing-strategy.md` and walk in with a range in mind. **Do not quote these on the call.** They set expectations, not prices.

| Form answer | Reads as | Points to |
|---|---|---|
| QuickBooks Online, Live | Segment A, pays Intuit for bookkeeping | Bill-to-bill anchor. Price at or above QB Live. |
| QuickBooks Online, Advanced | High spend band | Growth or Complex monthly tier |
| QuickBooks Online, Plus | Mid-High band, hit by the 2026 increase | Solo or Growth monthly tier |
| Simple Start or Essentials | Standard band | Solo tier |
| Months behind: 1 to 3 | Light cleanup | Roughly one month of the eventual monthly tier per month of backlog |
| Months behind: 6 to 12 or more than a year | Deep cleanup | Same rule of thumb, and expect the ladder to start at cleanup |
| Standing: "a mess I inherited" | Cleanup-first, high intent | Land on the fix, expand to monthly |
| Standing: "fine, off my plate" | Monthly-first, low urgency | Skip the cleanup pitch, sell the recurring |
