## Core Positioning Statement

Fenton Bookkeeping is standing delivery capacity for small QuickBooks-centric firms that have
promised their clients more than their own hours can deliver. One 30-year QuickBooks Certified
ProAdvisor holds a retainer relationship with at most a few firms (three in Package B —
`[count pending principal decision]`), stays warm on each firm's
context, and takes the episodic, high-stakes file — the cleanup, the catch-up, the migration
casualty — in the exact month it would otherwise break a public commitment. Fenton is never
client-facing: the firm's brand, the firm's review, the firm's client relationships.

**The mechanism:** the retainer buys **access and readiness** — warm context and a guaranteed
start window — and the episodic file work is billed on top at a retained rate. A temp arrives
cold every time; that re-briefing overhead is why temp hours are cheap and why they cannot be
trusted with a credentialed file. A standing relationship pays the context cost once. That
readiness is the one thing an offshore hour cannot replicate.

## Beachhead (the anchor market, chosen under constraint)

**Segment A of the WL-1 re-segmentation: capacity-trapped delivery firms** — QuickBooks-centric
practices, typically 2–15 people, that run monthly books under a visible commitment (an
advertised close date, a fixed-fee promise, a "100% on-time" claim) and separately sell lumpy
work (cleanup, catch-up) that collides with that calendar. Verified examples on the list
advertise exactly this collision on their own homepages.

**Why this segment first, honestly:** it is small (~3–4 of 17 site-checked WL-1 firms) but it is
the only segment whose pain is *observed rather than inferred*, and the session's strongest
empirical finding — the buyer's constraint is **time against a commitment**, not exhaustion and
not poaching — was found here. A hard capacity cap (three firms in Package B, count pending the
principal) does not
need a large beachhead; it needs three right ones.

## Value Pillars

### Pillar 1: The commitment holds ("you keep promising the 30th")

**Leads for:** firms with a public delivery promise and lumpy work that threatens it

The pitch is insurance against a visible commitment breaking: *you've promised the 30th; the
retainer is the reason you can keep promising it in the month a migration or cleanup lands.*
Episodic, high-stakes, credentialed work is precisely what cannot be hired for (no seat
justifies it), cannot go to a temp (arrives cold, rework lands on partner review), and cannot be
sourced cold once a client is already asking.

**Proof points:**
- The collision is observable on target firms' own sites (delivery promises + cleanup services
  sold side by side)
- **[PROOF GAP — capture on first firm engagement]** no delivered file yet; the first timed,
  reviewed engagement fills this slot

### Pillar 2: Work that survives partner review

**Leads for:** firms burned by outsourcing, or trust-first buyers (the persona's default)

The firm's name goes on the work, so the work is built to be reviewed: one named QuickBooks
Certified ProAdvisor with 30 years of books, payroll, reconciliation, and audit-support
experience — consistent files, defensible workpapers, the same person every engagement.
Structurally never client-facing: no client contact, no brand presence, nothing to poach with;
the model only works if Fenton stays invisible, and the firm can verify that incentive.

**Proof points:**
- ProAdvisor credential + 30 years (the credential is the market's proof that triple-digit
  rates clear — see pricing note below)
- Structural no-poach: not client-facing by design, verifiable in the engagement terms
- **[PROOF GAP]** no reference firm yet — start every relationship with one inspectable trial
  engagement (the persona's own expected buying motion)

### Pillar 3: Capacity that flexes, cost that doesn't compound

**Leads for:** firms weighing the hire they keep not making

A staff bookkeeper is $45–65K plus benefits carried year-round against seasonal peaks, months
away at best. Offshore hours are cheap but anonymous, cold on context, and the rework lands on
partner review time. The retainer-plus-file structure prices the middle: standing readiness at a
flat monthly fee, file work at a retained hourly rate, total cost tracking actual volume.
Capacity is capped at **three firms** `[pending principal decision — the cap is Package B's
number and rides on her real-solo-capacity input]` — scarcity is the honest size of a solo
practice holding warm context, but the count is hers to confirm.

**Proof points:**
- Hire math: salary + benefits + management overhead vs. retainer + measured file hours
- The cap itself: a promise-keeping number, not a growth ceiling pitch `[count pending
  principal decision]`

## Pricing posture (structure settled, numbers pending)

The offer structure **is** Package B of the 2026-07-27 pricing briefing (the briefing itself
was deleted 2026-07-29 as a dated memo; the structure it defined is canonical here): a monthly firm
retainer with **no included hours** (readiness, never prepaid labor), a 3-month minimum, a
retained project rate a shallow step below standard, a paid standalone diagnostic as the front
door, and a hard 3-firm cap.

**No number from that briefing is quoted outward until the principal's five inputs land and the
two flags resolve** (the $425/week figure; the untimed 20–40hr cleanup range). `[pending
principal decision]` is the provenance marker on every dollar figure in this
set. The Copy Rules forbid quoting unverified numbers; hours × rate, never fixed-fee, until one
real file is timed.

## What Not to Say

**Never the Bounce arguments.** No Bench shutdown, no QuickBooks Live backlog, no Intuit Assist
complaints, no August 1 price rise as an urgency hook. This buyer resells bookkeeping; they are
not shopping for it. If a WL message leans on the SMB radar, the two sets have been mixed.

**Never "I'll take work off your plate."** The verified constraint is time against a
*commitment*, not workload. Pitch the commitment holding, not relief from effort.

**Never compete on rate with offshore.** That comparison is a written loss condition. Concede
the rate, sell the readiness, walk away from pure rate-card evaluations.

**Never imply client contact.** Anything that smells like a competitor fishing for clients gets
deleted unread by this persona. The invisibility is the product.

**Never "certified ProAdvisors run your books" plural, and never claim the list is
QuickBooks-verified.** One person is the offer; and only 3 of 20 sampled WL-1 accounts carry a
confirmed ProAdvisor status — the list must not be described as certified.
