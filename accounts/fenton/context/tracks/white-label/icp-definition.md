# ICP Definition — White-Label Accounting Campaign

*Separate on purpose from `context/icp-definition.md` (the QuickBooks Bounce ICP). Different buyer, different list, different signals — never mix the two. Canonical for the White-Label campaign only (`outputs/campaigns/white-label-accounting/`).*

Last updated: 2026-07-13

---

## Ideal Customer Profile

**Who we sell to:** US accounting, CPA, and bookkeeping firms with real client rosters and constrained delivery capacity, who resell Fenton's QuickBooks work (cleanup, monthly bookkeeping, payroll) under their own brand. Fenton stays invisible — "not client-facing, we don't compete for relationships."

### Firmographics

| Dimension | Ideal Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Firm size | 2–50 people | Sweet spot, not a cap (same rule as Bounce: size preference lives in scoring points, never a filter). 2+ means there are clients to delegate; small enough that delivery capacity, not sales, is the bottleneck. Solo CPAs qualify **if** they have an overflow roster. |
| Type | Privately Held / Partnership | Independent firms decide fast; no procurement layer |
| Geography | US, nationwide | Same timezone-window logic as Bounce for calls (Miriam outside 7am–3pm PT) |
| Revenue | ~$200K–$10M | Wide on purpose; capacity pain exists across the band |

### Practice-fit (the technographic equivalent)

**Strong fit:**
- **QuickBooks-centric practice** — client books live in QBO; Fenton plugs in with zero platform translation. ProAdvisor-directory listing is a direct marker.
- Offers bookkeeping/CAS (client accounting services) as a line item — they already sell what we deliver

**Weak fit:**
- **Tax-led practices where bookkeeping is a line item** — *demoted from strong fit 2026-07-27:*
  the WL-1 site visits found these firms (segment D) have no bookkeeping-capacity problem to
  solve; they inflate list size and depress response. Override only on **observable standalone
  bookkeeping-capacity pain** (hiring for a bookkeeping role, waitlist language on the
  bookkeeping service specifically) — the general "cleanup season overflow" wedge did not
  survive contact with the actual sites.
- Practice standardized on Xero/Sage/proprietary stacks — translation cost kills the "behind the scenes" promise
- Already runs an offshore/outsourced delivery arm — the capacity slot is filled

### Organizational signals
- Actively hiring bookkeepers/staff accountants (capacity constraint made public)
- "Not accepting new clients" / waitlist language on the site
- One or two partners doing both selling and delivery (the classic ceiling)

## Tier sketch (firm counts unknown until sourcing runs — structure only)

| Tier | Criteria | Approach |
|------|----------|----------|
| 1 | QB-centric + active capacity signal (hiring, waitlist) | Hand-researched, personalized |
| 2 | QB-centric, right size band, no confirmed signal | Sequenced |
| 3 | Accounting firm, QB posture unconfirmed | Light-touch until enriched |
| 4 | Monitor | Off-stack firms, franchise offices |

## Anti-ICP

| Exclusion | Reason |
|-----------|--------|
| Solo practitioners with no client roster to delegate | Nothing to white-label; they ARE the delivery |
| Franchise tax chains (H&R Block, Liberty, etc.) | Corporate-mandated process and vendors |
| Firms with an established offshore delivery operation | Capacity need already solved, and on price we lose |
| Enterprise/regional firms (50+ with dedicated ops) | Have internal leverage; procurement motion we're not built for |
| Non-US firms | US tax/payroll context |
| **Fenton's own Bounce prospects/clients** | An SMB being pitched direct can never also be reached through a firm — channel-conflict suppression both ways |

## Qualification (discovery)

**Must-have:** real client roster · sells (or wants to sell) bookkeeping/CAS · comfortable with QBO delivery
**Strong:** hiring for delivery roles · waitlist/capacity language · tax-season overflow pattern · ProAdvisor listing
**Red flag:** wants to resell below viable delivery cost · demands on-site/in-person · wants Fenton client-facing
