## Value proposition

> For small QuickBooks firms that have promised clients more than their own hours can deliver,
> Fenton Bookkeeping is the retained ProAdvisor who takes the cleanup or migration file in the
> month it would break the promise, under the firm's brand and review, unlike offshore hours,
> which arrive cold every time and put the rework back on the partner.

One sentence. No em dash, no semicolon (Copy Rules, `context/positioning.md` — punctuation
discipline is shared across both sets even though the messages never are).

## Pillar 1 — The commitment holds

*The skeptic must believe: when the lumpy file lands in a close week, the close still happens.*

- The collision is on the target firms' own homepages: on-time delivery promised across dozens
  of books, cleanup sold as a separate service, same weeks, same hours.
- The verified buyer constraint is time against a commitment. The retainer sells a guaranteed
  start window, not a pool of hours.
- Episodic, high-stakes, credentialed work is the shape that defeats all three alternatives:
  no seat justifies the hire, no temp survives the review, no cold vendor starts fast enough.
- **[PROOF GAP]** No delivered engagement. First timed file fills this.

## Pillar 2 — Work that survives your review

*The skeptic must believe: putting their name on this work is safe, and their clients are safe.*

- One named QuickBooks Certified ProAdvisor, 30 years across books, payroll, reconciliations,
  and audit support. The same person on every file, producing workpapers built to be reviewed.
- Structurally never client-facing: no contact, no brand presence, nothing to poach with. The
  entire model depends on staying invisible, and the firm can verify that incentive.
- Trust is bought in steps, not claimed: every relationship starts with one inspectable trial
  engagement, which is how this persona already buys.
- **[PROOF GAP]** No reference firm. Do not fabricate one.

## Pillar 3 — Cost that tracks volume, not headcount

*The skeptic must believe: this is cheaper than the hire and safer than the cheap hour.*

- The hire runs $45–65K plus benefits and management, carried through the trough months. The
  retainer plus retained-rate file work rises and falls with what actually lands.
- Offshore is conceded on rate, on purpose. Cold context means re-briefing overhead and rework
  on partner time, which is the scarce resource the firm is protecting.
- Capacity is hard-capped at a small number of firms `[the count — three in Package B — pends
  the principal's real-solo-capacity input]`. Scarcity is stated, not implied. It is a
  promise-keeping number.
- **[DEFERRED]** Every dollar figure pends the principal's pricing decision. Nothing is quoted outward.

## Ladder-up check

Pillar 1 proves *takes the file in the month it would break the promise*. Pillar 2 proves *under
the firm's brand and review*. Pillar 3 proves *unlike offshore hours arriving cold*. No orphans.

## Per-channel projection

Same three pillars everywhere. Different length, never a different message. All surfaces below
are drafts-to-be until the pricing decision lands; none are live today.

| Surface | Carries | Copy |
|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn connection note** (the list is LinkedIn-ready, invite-capped) | Pillar 1 | *Your site promises books by the 30th. Curious what happens the month a cleanup lands on top of that.* |
| **Touch 1, angle `commitment-month`** | Pillar 1 | *Firms that promise a fixed close date carry an invisible liability: the month a rebuild file lands on top of it. Most absorb it in partner hours billed at nothing.* |
| **Touch 1, angle `hire-math`** | Pillar 3 | *A staff bookkeeper is 45 to 65K carried through the slow months. Most small firms' overflow is seasonal, which is why the seat never gets approved.* |
| **Touch 1, angle `review-survives`** | Pillar 2 | *Outsourced books usually fail at partner review, not at the ledger. Anonymous hours arrive cold, and the rework lands back on the reviewer.* |
| **Discovery call opener** | All three | *Walk me through the last month a cleanup or catch-up file landed during your close. What gave?* |
| **The 14-conversation test** (practitioner-to-practitioner, not a pitch) | Pillar 1 | *Is migration cleanup work you turn away, and what do you charge when you take it?* |

**PVP check on every angle:** strip the CTA and each touch still teaches the firm something
about its own economics (the commitment liability, the hire math, where outsourced work actually
fails). Per Copy Rules: attributed claims, no links, one CTA, under 120 words, no em or en
dashes in outbound copy, no urgency hooks.

## Known gaps (recorded, not papered over)

1. **No customer proof under any pillar.** Every proof point is a credential, a mechanism, or an
   observation about the buyer's own market. First firm engagement fills three slots at once.
2. **Pillar 3 carries no Fenton number, by decision.** Structure is settled (Package B); every
   figure pends the principal's five inputs and the two flags ($425/week provenance, the untimed
   20–40hr range).
3. **The list is not verified QuickBooks-native.** 3 of 20 sampled accounts carry ProAdvisor
   status. No claim about the recipient's stack in any copy until the cross-reference finishes.
4. **~15% list decay at the head.** Verification pass before any send wave.
