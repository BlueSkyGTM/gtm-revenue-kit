## Status Quo (DIY in QuickBooks Online)

**One-line summary:** Not a company — the default "competitor" for nearly every account on the current list is simply staying as-is: owner or admin manages the books directly inside QBO.
**Market position:** The overwhelming majority default
**Pricing:** Whatever their current QBO subscription costs — $35–$275+/mo depending on tier, post-2026 increases
**Typical buyer:** N/A — this is the absence of a decision, not a competitor's customer

**Their strengths (be honest):**
- Zero switching cost or new vendor relationship required
- Owner retains full control and visibility over every transaction
- "Already paying for it" — sunk cost makes inertia the path of least resistance

**Their weaknesses:**
- Owner's time is the true cost, and it's invisible on a P&L
- Errors compound silently until tax time or an audit surfaces them
- Now includes Intuit Assist's forced AI layer, which several users report miscoding transactions and making unauthorized changes

**Why customers switch to us:**
- A specific, nameable pain event: a price shock, a caught AI error, or hours lost fixing something that shouldn't have broken
- Realizing the "free" DIY option costs real hours every month that could go back into the business

**How to handle "we already do it ourselves" objection:**
> "Makes sense — a lot of owners we talk to do too, right up until QuickBooks' AI recodes something it shouldn't have, or the renewal bill jumps again. What's it actually costing you in hours each month right now?"

**Signal that an account uses them:**
- No internal bookkeeper/accountant role visible, active QBO subscription, no mention of an outsourced bookkeeping vendor

**Competitive message for Tier 1 outreach:**
> "QuickBooks just got more expensive and less predictable at the same time. Worth 15 minutes to see what it costs to hand the books to someone instead of fighting the software every month?"

## QuickBooks Live (Intuit's own bookkeeping add-on)

**One-line summary:** Intuit's in-house "human bookkeeper" service, bundled on top of a QuickBooks Online subscription
**Market position:** Default incumbent option for QBO subscribers who search for help without leaving the Intuit ecosystem
**Pricing:** $300–$700/mo (Full-Service, based on monthly expense volume) plus the separate QBO subscription ($35–$235+/mo); typically a $500–$800 mandatory cleanup fee before service starts; a cheaper "Expert Assisted" guidance-only tier exists at ~$59/mo
**Typical buyer:** QBO subscribers who want a bookkeeper but default to staying inside Intuit's product suite rather than researching alternatives

**Their strengths (be honest):**
- Native integration — works directly inside the QBO file the customer already has, no new login or handoff
- Often cheaper on paper than hiring a dedicated local bookkeeper

**Their weaknesses:**
- Owned by the same company causing the price increases and pushing the AI features customers are frustrated with
- Reviews report bookkeepers running 4–5 months behind on updates throughout the year
- No consistent point of contact — customers report getting a different bookkeeper most months, with no institutional knowledge carried over
- One documented case: a customer was charged a $1,500 cleanup fee to fix errors QuickBooks Live's own bookkeepers made
- Doesn't cover payroll, bill pay, or tax planning — narrower scope than it appears

**Why customers switch to us:**
- One consistent, named bookkeeper (30+ years experience) instead of a rotating, anonymous pool
- Not the same company that just raised their subscription price and shipped the AI features causing errors in the first place
- Payroll included as part of the service, not excluded

**How to handle "we're already using QuickBooks Live" objection:**
> "How's the turnaround been? A lot of QB Live customers we hear from end up months behind because they get handed off between bookkeepers. With us it's one person the whole time, and they actually know your books."

**Signal that an account uses them:**
- Mentions "QuickBooks Live" in job postings, reviews, or discovery conversation
- Complaints about being behind on reconciliation or inconsistent bookkeeper assignment

**Competitive message for Tier 1 outreach:**
> "If you're on QuickBooks Live, you've probably noticed you don't always get the same bookkeeper twice. We're one person, every month, who actually knows your books."

## Bench Accounting

**One-line summary:** National outsourced-bookkeeping brand, shut down abruptly on December 27, 2024 leaving 12,000+ businesses without access to their records, acquired by Employer.com three days later and relaunched under it in January 2025 *(year corrected 2026-08-03 — an earlier draft said 2026; coverage dates the relaunch to January 2025)*
**Market position:** Formerly a category leader; now rebuilding trust
**Pricing:** [inferred/reported: plans restarting around $189–$499+/mo depending on tier]
**Typical buyer:** Small businesses wanting a fully outsourced, software-plus-human bookkeeping solution — historically DTC/self-serve signup, low-touch relationship

**Their strengths (be honest):**
- National brand recognition, was a category leader pre-shutdown
- Own proprietary software layered on top of the bookkeeping service

**Their weaknesses:**
- December 2024 shutdown cut off 12,000+ businesses from their own financial records and tax documents with no warning
- Post-relaunch (2026) holds a D- BBB rating and is not BBB-accredited
- Ongoing complaints about late filings and unresponsive support since the Employer.com acquisition
- Acquired by an HR tech company with no prior bookkeeping experience — continuity of expertise is an open question

**Why customers switch to us:**
- A single, accountable, named person doing the work — not a platform that can disappear overnight
- 30+ years of direct bookkeeping experience vs. an organization rebuilding institutional knowledge post-acquisition

**How to handle "aren't you basically Bench?" objection:**
> "Different model — Bench is a software platform with bookkeeping staff behind it, which is part of why it was able to shut down overnight in 2024 and leave people locked out of their own books. This is one experienced person doing the work directly, and standing behind it."

**Signal that an account uses them:**
- Was a Bench customer before December 2024, or mentions researching Bench/reading about the shutdown

**Competitive message for Tier 1 outreach:**
> [Use only if relevant/raised by the prospect — don't lead unprompted] "A lot of people looking at outsourced bookkeeping right now remember what happened with Bench. Worth knowing who you're actually trusting with your books — that's why it's one person here, not a platform."

## The Independent Local Bookkeeper (added 2026-08-03)

**One-line summary:** The other solo or small-shop bookkeeper in the prospect's town or network — the alternative most able to copy the "one accountable person" claim, because they *are* one accountable person.
**Market position:** The most common alternative a prospect actually considers after deciding to stop DIY; found through referral, a CPA's recommendation, or a local search
**Pricing:** Commonly $40–$120/hr or roughly $200–$600/mo retainers for small files — often at or below Fenton's likely range
**Typical buyer:** An owner who wants a person, not a platform, and asks around before searching nationally

**Their strengths (be honest):**
- Every trust claim Fenton leads with — named person, accountability, continuity — is available to them word for word; nothing in "one accountable person" excludes them
- Referral-borne: they arrive pre-trusted through someone the owner already knows
- Local presence and face-to-face availability, which some owners value and Fenton does not offer
- Often cheaper, with lower overhead and grandfathered rates for long-standing clients

**Their weaknesses:**
- Quality varies enormously and is unverifiable from the outside — no consistent credential floor; the ProAdvisor certification plus 30 years is a real differentiator against the median but not the best of them
- Many are at capacity and slow to take new clients, or keep the books in their own software rather than the client's file
- Typically no structured intake, no documented process, no independent verification artifacts — service quality rests on the individual, invisibly
- Rarely equipped for a platform migration; QuickBooks-native shops have to recommend staying (the dual-capability wedge)

**Why customers switch to us:**
- The credential floor is provable up front (certification, 30 years) instead of discovered over a year of service
- Work happens inside the client's own QBO file with documented process and approval gates — the client keeps everything if they ever leave
- Dual capability: an independent view on stay-vs-leave that a QuickBooks-only local shop structurally cannot give

**How to handle "my neighbor/CPA recommended someone local" objection:**
> "A good local bookkeeper is a fine choice, and if they're taking clients and keeping your books in your own file, that can work well. The questions worth asking either of us: what's the certification, who owns the file, and what happens to your records if you ever part ways. We put those answers in writing up front."

**Signal that an account uses them:**
- Mentions "our bookkeeper" without naming a firm; books show consistent third-party activity without a service brand attached

**Honest note for copy:** never claim the independent local bookkeeper is inferior as a class — the differentiation is the *provable* floor (credential, process, file ownership, verification), not the category. Copy that only says "one accountable person" does not differentiate against them at all.

## Migration offer track — a different alternative set (added 2026-07-27)

*IMPACT step 1 for the migration offer (`playbooks/impact-positioning.md`). **Same buyer,
different question.** Everything above answers "who else would do your books?" This section
answers "how else would you leave QuickBooks?" — and the alternatives barely overlap. Never mix
the two sets in one message; the bookkeeping offer promises nothing changes, this one moves
them.*

### Status quo — stay on QBO and absorb the increase

**The default, and the one to beat.** Essentials $75→$85, Plus $115→$140, Advanced $275→$340
on the account's next billing date.
**Their strengths (honestly):** zero effort, zero risk, no retraining, no data exposure. The
increase is real but survivable for most, and Intuit softened Advanced with bundled value
(Bill Pay Elite, Workforce). Switching costs are highest exactly where the increase is biggest,
so the tiers that got hit hardest churn least.
**Where we win:** they can name the number on their own invoice and it annoys them enough to
ask what the alternative costs — especially with a user-cap problem forcing a tier they don't
need.
**How to handle it:** never argue that they *should* be angry. Show the destination dollars on
both platforms and let the arithmetic talk.

### DIY migration with the free tooling — the strongest rival

**Q2X** (Xendoo; Xero's preferred US partner since 2026-03-17; base conversion free,
Xero-subsidized $250 value, full transaction history, ~20-minute eligibility call) and
**Jet Convert** (free, current + prior fiscal year). Desktop must stage through QBO first;
Enterprise unsupported; US-only.
**Their strengths (honestly):** it is genuinely free, genuinely supported by the vendor, and
for a clean small file with shallow history it is genuinely enough. They also validate their
own conversions.
**Their weaknesses:** the tool moves data, it does not judge accounting consequences — payroll
YTD does not transfer at all, chart mapping is a judgment call, integrations must be
reconnected, and nobody independent tells the owner whether the result is *right*. When
something is off, the owner discovers it at month-end.
**Where we win:** they have a messy file, real history, payroll, or connected apps — and no
appetite to be their own project manager and their own auditor.
**⚠ Channel-capture risk, stated plainly:** **Xendoo runs its own bookkeeping practice.** A
prospect sent to their eligibility call may be cross-sold Xendoo's bookkeeping before Fenton
captures the retainer. Mitigations: establish the Fenton relationship and diagnostic *before*
any hand-off, and prefer **Jet Convert** where history depth allows, since it has no
bookkeeping arm.

### A Xero-side firm

Xero-certified bookkeepers and migration-badge holders.
**Their strengths (honestly):** they know the destination well, some hold the Migration
specialist badge, and the Xero advisor directory sends them inbound.
**Their weaknesses:** they know where you are going, not what you are leaving. QuickBooks
quirks (how Live coded things, what Intuit Assist changed, how payroll items were set up) are
exactly where migrations break, and that is origin-side knowledge.
**Where we win:** the buyer's fear is losing history and getting the accounting wrong, not
learning new software. A guide who knows the origin *and* can judge the destination is a
narrower field.

### Another QuickBooks-native bookkeeper who also learns Xero (added 2026-08-03)

The nearest real competitor, per the migration red-team (F7): a bookkeeper with the same
origin-side knowledge who adds destination competence — nothing structural stops them.
**Their strengths (honestly):** identical origin knowledge; if they are local and
referral-borne they arrive pre-trusted; the "knows what you are leaving" claim is theirs too.
**Their weaknesses:** dual-platform competence is real work to acquire and maintain, and
almost none advertise it today; none carry an independent verification artifact.
**Where we win:** being *demonstrably* dual-capable now — the verifier, the proof packet, the
documented rehearsal — versus a claim any shop could make but few can evidence. This is why
the proof artifacts are the moat, not the phrase "we know both systems."

### Switch to something else entirely

Wave, FreshBooks, or a Xero competitor.
**Their strengths (honestly):** cheaper still, and for a very simple business genuinely
sufficient.
**Their weaknesses:** a different product class — less accountant support, thinner ecosystems,
and often a harder path back if the business grows.
**Where we win:** anyone with payroll, inventory, multi-user needs, or an accountant in the
loop. This alternative mostly self-selects out.

### What we do NOT claim against these

**Not "nobody else verifies migrations"** — Q2X advertises validation of its own conversions.
The honest claim is **independence**: an evidence-backed reconciliation from someone who is not
the vendor grading its own work, handed to the client to keep. `[PROOF GAP]` until the sandbox
rehearsal and the first delivered file exist.
