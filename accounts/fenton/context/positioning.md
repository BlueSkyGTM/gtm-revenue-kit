## Core Positioning Statement

Fenton Bookkeeping is done-for-you QuickBooks bookkeeping for small business owners who are done paying more for software and getting less certainty in return. One experienced human — not a rotating team, not an AI feature — reconciles your books, catches what's wrong, and keeps it that way every month.

**The mechanism:** Every target account is already a QuickBooks Online subscriber, which means there's no platform migration, no new software to learn, and no workflow disruption. The switch is entirely about who's doing the work behind the screen — a QuickBooks Certified ProAdvisor with 30+ years of experience, instead of the account owner themselves or an AI feature that keeps making changes they didn't ask for.

**The only-statement (added 2026-08-03 — what survives against each alternative, not just
QuickBooks Live):** against **DIY**, the structural limit is that the owner's books are checked
by the person who made the entries — there is no independent second reader, and the owner's
hours are the invisible cost; naming that limit is the claim, not "we're professionals."
Against **QuickBooks Live**, independence: the help does not come from the company raising the
price and running the AI. Against **the independent local bookkeeper** — who can say "one
accountable person" word for word — the differentiation is the **provable floor**: certification
and 30 years stated up front, work inside the client's own file with a documented approval
process, and the client keeps everything if they ever leave. Copy that only says "one
accountable person" differentiates against nobody local; copy must carry at least one plank of
the provable floor.

### Pillar 2: Predictable Cost vs. an Escalating Subscription

**Leads for:** Budget-conscious owners who just absorbed the 2026 QuickBooks Online price increase

QuickBooks Online raised subscription prices again in 2026, part of a repeated pattern of increases since 2021, and the bill keeps climbing regardless of whether the owner uses any of the new features. We offer a flat, predictable cost for someone to actually do the work, instead of an ever-rising bill for software you still have to operate yourself.

**Proof points:**
- Verified pricing (re-verified 2026-08-03, after the increase took effect): **current list price is Simple Start $38, Essentials $85, Plus $140, Advanced $340.** The Aug 1, 2026 increase landed at the projected numbers (from $75/$115/$275 on the three raised tiers). Verified against Intuit's Firm of the Future announcement and secondary coverage (Certum Solutions, Insightful Accountant); the intuit.com pricing page itself blocks automated reads, so a manual spot-check there before quoting in new copy stays the standing rule. Note: an increase applies at each account's **renewal** on or after Aug 1, so a given prospect's own bill reprices on their billing date, not on Aug 1 — this is what drives the per-account eligibility rule. Anchor Fenton's flat fee against the prospect's actual bill.

### Pillar 3: No More Fighting the Software

**Leads for:** Owners specifically frustrated with Intuit Assist's forced automation and lack of an opt-out

## What Not to Say

**Don't lead with "white-label" or "accounting firm" language in current outreach** — that's the future motion (selling capacity to accounting/CPA firms), not the current one. The active list is small business owners, and messaging built for firms (e.g., "not client-facing," "integrates into your workflow") will read as confusing or off-target to them.

**Don't frame this as "AI vs. human" as an abstract debate** — the AI-frustration angle only works when tied to a specific, real complaint (miscoded transactions, unwanted changes, unreliable reports). Leading with a generic "AI can't be trusted" line sounds like a talking point, not a solved problem.

**Don't promise savings versus a hire without doing the math for that specific account** — "cheaper than hiring a bookkeeper" is directionally true but should be backed by an actual comparison (their likely retainer cost vs. a bookkeeper's salary + benefits + management overhead), not stated as a blanket claim.

## Copy Rules (hardened — enforce on every outbound email)

These are non-negotiable. Outbound copy is generated through a linter that fails the build on any violation (see `outputs/campaigns/*/` generator scripts).

**PVP across the board.** Every email — every tier, every segment — must pass the Permissionless Value Prop test from `skills/signal-to-sequence/SKILL.md`: delete the final CTA line and the body must still state something true and useful about *their* books. Insider knowledge beats a cold pitch. We do not send pitch-template copy, even to low-priority segments.

**No em dashes. No en dashes.** Use a period, a comma, or a colon instead. This is the single most common AI tell and it is banned outright.

**No AI-artifact vocabulary.** Banned words/phrases include: delve, leverage, elevate, streamline, robust, seamless, unlock, empower, navigate, landscape, realm, tapestry, testament, "in today's", "fast-paced", "at the end of the day", "it's worth noting", "rest assured", "game-changer", "cutting-edge", "when it comes to", "that said", moreover, furthermore, additionally, crucial, vital, pivotal, myriad, plethora, "not just X but Y", "whether you're". Also no semicolons and no emoji. Write the way one business owner talks to another.

**Grounding + attribution unchanged.** AI-behavior claims are always attributed ("owners have been posting…"), never asserted about the recipient's account. Prices as destination dollars, never percentages. No links. One CTA. Under 120 words.

**NO URGENCY OR DEADLINE PRESSURE (operator direction, 2026-07-25, in force until lifted).** Do not build copy around the Aug 1 2026 Intuit price increase, or any other dated event, as a reason to act now. The variant that did this ("the August 1 QuickBooks increase") was removed from the live campaign.

Two things this rule does **not** mean:
- The price increase is still **true and still useful as market context and as a targeting signal.** Plus/Advanced subscribers remain the highest-spend, best-fit segment, and `context/icp-definition.md` and `context/signal-library.md` correctly keep using the tiers to prioritise. Keep that. Drop only the "act before the deadline" framing.
- It is not a permanent ban. Revisit when the operator is comfortable with it.

Why it was dropped, so the reasoning survives the decision: a shared, widely-announced price rise is not a reason to trust a stranger. Every recipient already got the same notice from Intuit, and every bookkeeper in the country is mailing about it the same week, so a deadline hook competes in the most crowded inbox moment of the year. Worse for this list specifically, **55% of it already pays Intuit for QuickBooks Live** — they have already chosen to outsource and pay a brand, which makes "switch to me before the price goes up" a harder ask, not an easier one.

**Preferred framing instead: a one-time cleanup.** It is a smaller, lower-trust purchase that suits a sender with no reference customers, it is compatible with scarce retainer capacity, and it converts the same AI-error insight into an offer rather than a warning.
