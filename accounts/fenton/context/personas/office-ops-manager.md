# Persona: Office / Ops Manager

**Titles:** Office Manager, Operations Manager, Administrative Manager, Executive Assistant handling finance tasks
**Decision role:** Champion — surfaces the problem and pushes for a solution, but the owner approves spend
**Found at:** Slightly larger small businesses in the ICP range (roughly 10–20 employees) where the owner has delegated day-to-day bookkeeping rather than doing it personally

---

## What They Care About

**Primary metric:** Their own workload and whether the owner trusts the numbers they hand over. Bookkeeping is one piece of a broader role (often HR, admin, and ops combined) — it's rarely the part of their job they were hired to be great at.

**Their biggest problem right now:** QuickBooks now takes more of their time than it used to — either because they're double-checking what Intuit Assist changed, or because reconciling has gotten more confusing since the AI features were added. They're doing this alongside everything else on their plate, and if something's wrong in the books, it reflects on them even though bookkeeping isn't their core skill.

**What a good week looks like for them:** Books close on time, nothing gets flagged by the owner or the accountant, and they spent an hour on it instead of a full afternoon.

**What a bad week looks like:** The owner asks why a number looks off, and they have to spend real time figuring out whether it was their error or the software's — while everything else on their desk waits.

---

## How They Buy

**Involvement:** Influences and champions internally — surfaces the pain to the owner and makes the recommendation, but doesn't sign off on spend alone
**Discovery behavior:** [inferred] Likely to be the one who actually reads outreach email or answers the phone before the owner ever sees it — screens on the business's behalf
**Evaluation style:** Practical — cares whether it will genuinely reduce their workload and whether the transition is easy, more than the underlying philosophy of "why QuickBooks AI is untrustworthy"
**Timeline:** Depends on how quickly they can get the owner's buy-in — usually fast once they've decided it's worth raising, since the owner is a single approver, not a committee

**Common objections:**
- "I don't want to hand this off and lose visibility" → "You'd still see everything — this doesn't take away access, it takes away the hours you spend catching AI mistakes."
- "The owner won't want to spend more" → Frame it as a swap, not an addition: less time on bookkeeping vs. a predictable flat cost, easy to bring to the owner as a straightforward tradeoff.

---

## How to Reach Them

**Best channel:** Email or phone — they're often the first point of contact for a small business, so outreach addressed generically may reach them before the owner
**Best time:** [inferred] Standard business hours, since this is part of their working role rather than something handled off-hours like the owner might
**What gets their attention:** A clear, specific time-savings framing — not a pitch about AI trust in the abstract, but "this is the thing taking up your Tuesday afternoons"
**What gets ignored:** Messaging pitched purely at ownership-level cost savings — that's not their frame; they respond to workload relief

---

## Message Framework

**Value prop for this persona:** Get bookkeeping off your desk entirely — hand it to one experienced person, keep full visibility, and stop being the one who has to catch QuickBooks' AI mistakes before the owner does.

**Proof points that resonate:**
- QuickBooks Certified ProAdvisor, 30+ years of experience — someone who can be trusted with the handoff without hand-holding
