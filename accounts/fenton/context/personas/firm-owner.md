# Persona: Firm Owner / Managing Partner (White-Label campaign)

**Titles:** Owner, Managing Partner, Principal, President, CPA (firm principal), Founder
**Decision role:** Champion and economic buyer in one — small-firm structure, decides alone or with one partner
**Found at:** Independent US accounting/CPA/bookkeeping firms, 2–50 people, QuickBooks-centric practice
**Campaign:** White-Label Accounting ONLY — never use this persona for the Bounce list

---

## What They Care About

**Primary metric:** Realized rate per hour and client retention. Every hour a partner spends on bookkeeping is an hour not spent on tax strategy, advisory, or selling — the work they bill 3–5x more for.

**Their biggest problem right now:** They sell more than they can deliver. Client books pile up between deadlines; the choice is turn away revenue, hire (slow, risky, payroll they carry year-round for seasonal peaks), or let quality slip. Bookkeeping is their lowest-margin service but the stickiest client relationship — they can't drop it without losing the tax work attached to it.

**What a good month looks like:** Client books close on time without a partner touching them; the firm takes on two new clients without a hiring conversation.

**What a bad month looks like:** A staff bookkeeper quits in February. Partners do reconciliations at night during filing season. A client notices their books are three months behind and starts shopping.

---

## How They Buy

**Involvement:** Decides directly; may consult the other partner. No procurement.
**Discovery behavior:** Peer referrals (state societies, Intuit ProAdvisor circles, tax Twitter/forums), or when a hiring attempt fails.
**Evaluation style:** Trust-first, then a paid test. They will not hand a client's books to a stranger — expect a trial engagement (one cleanup or one client's monthly) before any volume.
**Timeline:** Slow to first yes, fast to expand. Tax-season pain (Jan–Apr) and extension season (Sep–Oct) are the buying windows.

**Common objections:**
- "How do I know the quality holds up under my name?" → The work is built to be reviewed: one experienced person (30-yr ProAdvisor), consistent files, defensible workpapers. Start with one engagement and inspect everything.
- "Will you poach my clients?" → Structurally impossible to poach and keep the model: Fenton is never client-facing, no client contact, no brand presence. The whole business depends on staying invisible.
- "I could hire instead" → A hire is $45–65K + benefits carried through the slow months. Capacity here flexes with your actual volume and disappears when you don't need it.
- "I already tried outsourcing and got burned" → Ask what broke: usually anonymous offshore teams and rework. This is one named person whose work the firm reviews, not a queue.

---

## How to Reach Them

**Best channel:** Email (they live in it), during their local early window per the Bounce timezone table. Avoid Jan 15–Apr 15 for cold *starts* — they're unreachable; land just before (Nov–Dec: "capacity for next season") or just after (May: the post-season exhaustion window).
**What gets their attention:** Capacity math in their own terms — realized rate, turnaway revenue, the February resignation scenario. Specific, checkable observations about their own firm (hiring posts, waitlist language).
**What gets ignored:** "Outsourced bookkeeping services" pitches (they get dozens, mostly offshore), anything implying client contact, anything that smells like a competitor fishing.

---

## Message Framework

**Value prop for this persona:** Sell more bookkeeping without hiring for it — one experienced ProAdvisor delivers under your brand, your review, your client relationships, invisible to your clients.

**Proof points that resonate:**
- Miriam: 30 years of books, QuickBooks Certified ProAdvisor, audit-support and cleanup background (the work survives review)
- Structural no-poach: never client-facing, by design
- [capture as they land] First firm engagement: turnaround time, error rate found in inherited books

**PVP note (per Copy Rules):** touch-1 must teach the firm something about its OWN capacity/economics even if they never reply — e.g. the realized-rate cost of partner-hours on bookkeeping, or what their own hiring post signals about the delivery ceiling. No links, one reply CTA, under 120 words, no em dashes, attributed claims.
